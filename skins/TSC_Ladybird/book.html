The quotes are the owner's own words, as they were typed. Every picture is the car as it stood at that step, drawn in the studio's viewer.

01

Brief

What the car is, agreed before anything was painted.

"there's many maps that most of the map consists of grass terrain … I do want something that feels fun and it just makes sense to use it when in maps like those"

Camouflage would have been the obvious answer, and the wrong one: on a green map you want to see your own car. So the studio started from things made to be picked out against grass: a jockey's colours, a golf flag, a ladybird on a leaf.

"maybe it is something that stands out, but I do wonder if the bottom is some lever of grass, that acts as the blend between the grass and the bug"

"Sure, ladybird. We could potentially have a family of bugs, but we could start with that"

The brief card

A ladybird in the grass, made for the grass maps and fun to drive there: the top of the car stands out against the green like a ladybird on a leaf, and the bottom rises out of a fringe of grass that blends it into the map. The first of what could become a family of bugs.

Drawn from

  • The ladybird: glossy red and black spots, nature's "look at me" against green.
  • A jockey's racing colours: bold shapes in two strong colours, picked out at a gallop.
  • The lawn at ground level: blades drawn as a clean, graphic edge.

Not

  • Camouflage, or green all over.
  • Grass as a photo, or grass anywhere but the bottom.
  • A toy or a cartoon bug: no eyes, antennae or face.

Fixed

  • A fringe of grass along the bottom, the blend between the map and the car.
02

Mood

The brief turned into a look before it touched the car.

Three boards were drawn, each its own finish, graphic language and colour story: Lacquer, Racing colours and Field guide. The owner picked the Field guide, then changed their mind and kept two.

"c and b actually"

03

Concepts

Three rough ideas on the car, the big shapes only.

One concept for each board, and one that blends them. Rough on purpose: flat colour, no details, the wheels and inner car one dark colour until their own steps.

"I like B the most. I do like the grass from C though"

So the car became B's racing silks with C's turf-green blades rising up the sides. The owner also caught a spot running down inside a side inlet. From then on the spots sit on the outer panels only, clear of the inlets and of the panels where the game prints the player's number and name.

04

Shapes

The big shapes made right, from the owner's notes pinned on the car.

"I think lady bug spots are not perfect circles, more like a blob close to being a circle"

"I wonder if we make this black? as if it's the head."

The spots became blobs, each edge wandering in a few slow lobes. The nose became the ladybird's black head, meeting the red shell along a curved edge, with a white mark on each side. The grass grew taller and denser. A spot that ran off the deck's edge moved in, and one that touched the cockpit went, the head taking its place.

The white marks raised a question: from the front they read a little like eyes, which the brief ruled out. They were shown two ways, round as asked or drawn out into streaks. The owner kept them round.

"The grass should be around the car"

The nose floats above the ground, so grass growing from the ground only reached it as stray tips. The head got its own fringe of short blades along its lower edge, and the flat plate under the nose became a lawn.

05

Colours and materials

How much each part shines. The colours stayed the same in all three.

The two boards disagreed about shine: the ladybird is glossy, a jockey's silks are satin, a field guide's paint is flat. All three were painted, with nothing else changed.

"a silky grass finish, so there's some contrast in the ladybug and the grass"

A, with one change: the ladybird glossy, the grass a silky satin.

06

Wheels

Covers, tyres, tread and the wheels' own light, as one piece.

Each wheel carries a small light that stays on day and night, cyan on the stock car. In each version it took the version's colour.

"C"

The only one of the three that puts the grass where the driver sees it: on the rear tyres, all race.

07

Details

The inner car, the lights and the glass.

The inner car went satin black, with glossy black frames round the side inlets and the speed display to match the head. The speed numbers and the gear lights on the tail turned grass green, because from behind they are what the driver looks at all race. Braking still turns the rear lights red. The glass stayed clear, since a tint would dim the lights behind it.

"probably this needs to be green"

Pinned on the front wing, and then on the strip along the bottom edge. The floor and the front wing went green, so the grass reaches the car's very edge.

08

Lettering

Each board had its own words. The owner chose none.

"c"

09

Review

The whole car against the brief and a quality list.

Every part painted, none left in the base clay. The spots, the grass, the head and its marks are clean wherever they meet a fold, a join or an edge. From the game's cameras the car reads as a ladybird at once, by day and at night. The panels where the game prints the player's number and name stay clear, and the game files come to about 3 MB, well inside the game's limit.

One fix: the small fins under the tail had caught the grass as green and red slivers. They went solid green, like the floor.

One note: seen from above and from the front, the head's two white marks read like a cartoon ladybird's eyes, which the brief ruled out. The owner chose them knowingly.

10

Road test

Skipped.

"Let's assume it works"

Two things only the game can show are still open: how the turf green sits next to the game's own grass, and how the white marks read at speed.

Release

The car, final.
